Service overview

From scattered indicators to an actionable groundwater picture

Groundwater occurrence is influenced by geology, geomorphology, slope, drainage, fractures, soil, rainfall and land use. No single data source fully characterises local conditions. VTS combines relevant evidence layers within a GIS framework to classify a study area into relative groundwater potential zones.

These maps support district and watershed planning, recharge screening, agricultural water management, baseline studies and prioritisation of field investigations.

Technical limitation: A groundwater potential map is a regional decision-support product. It does not establish guaranteed well yield or replace detailed hydrogeological and geophysical investigation for final borewell siting.
Our methodology

How the model is developed

The workflow is adapted to terrain, study scale, available datasets and the decision the map must support.

01 — Scope

Define the decision

Confirm study boundary, mapping scale, purpose, classification requirements and available ground observations.

02 — Data

Compile spatial inputs

Acquire suitable satellite imagery, DEM, rainfall, geology, geomorphology, soils and well records.

03 — Layers

Prepare thematic layers

Generate and quality-check slope, drainage density, lineament density and land-use layers in a consistent spatial reference.

04 — Model

Integrate evidence

Select weighted overlay, AHP, fuzzy methods or validated machine-learning methods according to study needs.

05 — Validate

Check against observations

Where available, compare predictions with well yield, water levels, borehole records and site evidence.

06 — Deliver

Translate findings into decisions

Produce classified maps, GIS datasets, area statistics, methodology, interpretation and limitations.

Frequently asked questions

Groundwater mapping FAQs

What is groundwater potential zone mapping?

It is a regional GIS-based assessment combining geological, geomorphological, terrain, drainage, rainfall, land use and, where available, field indicators to classify relative groundwater potential.

Which datasets are used?

Potential inputs include geology, geomorphology, lineament and drainage density, slope, soil, rainfall, land-use/land-cover, aquifer and well data. The final selection depends on local conditions and data quality.

Can this identify an exact borewell location?

No. Regional potential maps support screening and prioritisation, but final borewell siting requires site-specific hydrogeological and, where appropriate, geophysical investigation.

Can well records be used for validation?

Yes. Reliable well yield, groundwater level and borehole records can help test how well the classified zones reflect observed conditions.
